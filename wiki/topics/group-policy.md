# Group Policy

**The AD mechanism for centrally configuring computer and user settings: policy
lives in Group Policy Objects linked into the AD hierarchy and is applied by
client-side extensions at startup and sign-in.**

## Body

A **Group Policy Object (GPO)** is a named collection of settings, permissions, and
scope. It has two physical halves:
- the **Group Policy container** — stored in the **domain partition of AD** (and
  replicated by AD replication / [[ad-replication]]);
- the **Group Policy template** — files in the **SYSVOL** share on every DC,
  replicated by **FRS** (legacy) or **DFSR**.

Settings split into **Computer configuration** (system-wide — firewall, power, etc.,
applied at startup) and **User configuration** (per-user — folder redirection, etc.,
applied at sign-in).

**Scope is set by linking** a GPO to a **site, domain, or OU**. At startup/sign-in
the Group Policy service queries AD for the GPOs applicable to the object based on
its site, its domain, and the chain of OUs above it, then each **client-side
extension (CSE)** applies its slice of the resulting policy (registry, security,
etc.). Each GPO has a GUID and evaluation follows the AD hierarchy.

Authoring/management uses the **Local Group Policy Editor** (`gpedit.msc`) for local
settings or the **Group Policy Management Console (GPMC)** for domain GPOs; **Group
Policy Preferences** offer similar reach with different (preference, not enforced)
semantics. By default the **PDC Emulator** ([[fsmo-roles]]) is the DC the GPMC edits
against, which keeps a consistent edit target across admins (inferred — connects the
GP edit target to the FSMO role).

## Contradictions / caveats

- Because the container is in AD and the template is in SYSVOL replicated separately,
  the two halves can desync if SYSVOL (FRS/DFSR) replication is unhealthy — a GPO can
  appear in AD but not apply because its template didn't reach a DC.
