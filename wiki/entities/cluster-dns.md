# Cluster DNS

**Cluster DNS resolves internal Kubernetes/OpenShift service and pod names (`*.cluster.local`) for every pod, served by CoreDNS and managed on OpenShift by the DNS Operator.**

## How it works
- The **DNS Operator** deploys and manages a CoreDNS instance as a **daemon set**, creates a Service in front of it, and configures the **kubelet** to point pods' `/etc/resolv.conf` at that Service — enabling DNS-based Service discovery and resolution of `cluster.local` names.
- The Operator's `Status.Cluster Domain` field reports the base domain used to construct fully-qualified pod/service domain names (default `cluster.local`); `AVAILABLE` is `True` once at least one CoreDNS daemon-set pod is `Available` and the DNS Service has a ClusterIP.
- Every Service gets `*.<namespace>.svc.cluster.local` (see [[kubernetes-service]]); the operator's `spec.servers.zones` field cannot itself be set to `cluster.local` (reserved).

## Pod resolv.conf and search domains
- Kubernetes injects a `search` list (namespace, `svc.cluster.local`, cluster domain, plus any node-inherited search) and `options ndots:5` by default into each pod's DNS config, so a bare short name is tried against every search domain before falling back to an absolute query.
- Kubernetes caps search domains at **32 entries / 2048 total characters**; exceeding it truncates the list, which can silently break short-name resolution, and container runtimes may leave pods stuck if the list is malformed.

## OpenShift specifics vs upstream
- Upstream Kubernetes conceptually describes Pod/Service DNS behavior (`ndots`, search lists, DNS policies) generically; OpenShift additionally exposes this through the cluster-scoped **DNS Operator** custom resource (`dns.operator.openshift.io`), which is the OCP-specific management layer on top of the same CoreDNS mechanism (inferred synthesis of the two notes above).
