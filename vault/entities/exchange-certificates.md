# Exchange Digital Certificates

**Exchange installs self-signed certificates by default; production deployments
typically replace them with an internal-CA or commercial certificate covering all
externally- and internally-published names.**

## Body

Three certificate sources, each with tradeoffs
(`exchange-exchange-servertoc-p0241-0280.md:1274-1320`):

- **Self-signed** — free, but not automatically trusted by clients/devices (must be
  manually added to trusted-root stores where that's even possible), can't be
  revoked, and hard to lifecycle-manage.
- **Internal CA** (e.g. Active Directory Certificate Services) — cheaper than
  commercial, but same trust-distribution problem as self-signed, plus PKI
  operational overhead.
- **Commercial CA** — costs money but is automatically trusted by essentially
  every client/device/server out of the box; simplest to deploy at scale.

Three identity-matching methods
(`exchange-exchange-servertoc-p0241-0280.md:1326-1395`):

- **Subject match** — CN identifies exactly one host; simple but requires one
  certificate (and often one IP binding) per host.
- **Subject Alternative Name (SAN)** — one certificate lists multiple hostnames
  (e.g. `www.contoso.com`, `ftp.contoso.com`); convenient but every listed host
  shares fate — you can't selectively revoke one name without affecting the
  others.
- **Wildcard** (`*.contoso.com`) — covers any host at that subdomain level without
  enumerating names ahead of time, but cannot cross top-level domains, cannot
  cover a *different* subdomain level (`*.contoso.com` does not cover
  `www.eu.contoso.com`), isn't available with Extended Validation, and a
  compromise affects every host under the domain.

At install, Exchange 2016/2019/SE creates **two self-signed certificates** from
Exchange itself plus a **third** from Windows (the `WMSVC` cert used by IIS's Web
Management service); the Exchange-issued ones are used respectively for
general/default services (not trusted by clients out of the box) and for
server-to-server authentication — replacing the Microsoft Exchange self-signed
certificate is **not required purely to encrypt** traffic between Exchange servers
(`exchange-exchange-servertoc-p0241-0280.md:1397-1447`).
