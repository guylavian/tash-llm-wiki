# Pod Security Admission (PSA)

**PSA is the upstream Kubernetes admission controller that enforces the three Pod Security Standards levels (`privileged`, `baseline`, `restricted`) via per-namespace labels — distinct from and layered alongside OpenShift's own [[security-context-constraints]].**

## Modes and labels (upstream)
| Mode | Label | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `enforce` | `pod-security.kubernetes.io/enforce` | Rejects a non-compliant pod |
| `audit` | `pod-security.kubernetes.io/audit` | Logs an audit annotation, allows the pod |
| `warn` | `pod-security.kubernetes.io/warn` | User-facing warning, allows the pod |

Each mode can pin a specific Kubernetes minor version via a `-version` label suffix. Enforcement applies only to actual Pod objects, not to workload resources (Deployment/Job) that template them — audit/warn do apply to workload resources too, to catch violations early.

## OpenShift defaults (OpenShift-specific)
- Globally: `privileged` is **enforced**, `restricted` is used for **warn+audit** only.
- `default`, `kube-public`, and `kube-system` namespaces are permanently pinned to `privileged` and cannot be changed.
- A **synchronization controller** automatically sets each namespace's `warn`/`audit` labels to the most-privileged profile permitted by the SCCs available to that namespace's ServiceAccounts — so PSA warnings track SCC grants rather than requiring separate manual PSA labeling. Manually overriding a synced label disables sync for that label until removed or explicitly reconciled.
- Sync is permanently disabled on system namespaces and initially disabled on user-created `openshift-*` namespaces (can be enabled later).

## PSA vs SCC (OpenShift-specific, the coexistence model)
Two independent controllers reconcile in sequence:
1. The **SCC controller** may mutate certain fields (e.g. defaulting an empty `seccompProfile` to `RuntimeDefault` if the assigned SCC requires it), then validates the pod against its matched SCC.
2. The **PSA controller** then validates the (possibly SCC-mutated) pod against the namespace's PSA labels.

A pod can pass SCC admission and still be flagged/rejected by PSA (or vice versa in `warn`/`audit` mode) — they are enforced independently, not merged into one gate (inferred: this two-controller ordering is why "the SCC is fine but PSA still warns" support tickets occur).
