# PersistentVolumeClaim (PVC)

**A PVC is a user's request for storage — size, access mode(s), and volume mode — that a control-plane loop binds to a matching PersistentVolume (PV), which is then mounted into a pod via the Pod's `volumes` block.** The PV describes the storage backend's details (backend type, capacity, reclaim policy); the PVC is the consumer-facing abstraction that hides them.

## Access modes
A PV/PVC declares one or more of: **ReadWriteOnce (RWO)**, **ReadOnlyMany (ROX)**, **ReadWriteMany (RWX)**, or **ReadWriteOncePod (RWOP)** (single specific pod on the whole cluster, not just single node). A control loop finds a PV whose access modes and capacity satisfy the PVC's request.

## Binding and provisioning
- A pre-created PV binds to a matching PVC automatically once one exists that meets the request.
- If no matching PV exists and a [[storage-class]] is specified (or a default one applies), the class's provisioner **dynamically provisions** a new PV to satisfy the PVC (see [[csi-driver]] / dynamic provisioning). **A PVC with no matching PV and no viable StorageClass stays `Pending` indefinitely** — the most common storage-request failure mode.
- Deleting a PVC still in use by a pod does not remove it immediately; similarly deleting a PV bound to a live PVC does not remove it immediately (both wait out finalizers/active use) — deleting a resource out from under an active workload does not silently orphan storage (inferred, generalized from the individual delete-ordering notes for PV/PVC).

## Reclaim on release
When a PVC is deleted, what happens to its PV is governed by the PV's `persistentVolumeReclaimPolicy` — see [[storage-class]] for how the class sets the default reclaim policy on volumes it provisions.
